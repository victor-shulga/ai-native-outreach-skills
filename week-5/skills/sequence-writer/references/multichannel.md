# Running both channels

## Both channels on one person

Two positions exist, and both are right under different conditions.

**Against** (a common practitioner position, 2026): do not run both channels on
one person. You cannot tell what worked; LinkedIn becomes the bottleneck of the
whole sequence because its ceiling is a few hundred invites per profile; a
LinkedIn test takes about three weeks against six days in email.

**For** (the position this skill implements): until the list passes 10,000, write
in both. The expensive thing in outbound is a relevant list. If you sent 100
invites and 70 were not accepted, that is not a slow test — those are 70 relevant
people you never reached at all. Email reaches them.

What the numbers say:

- reply to a LinkedIn invite fell from 3.5% to 2.2% in a year, sample 13.2M invites
- invite accept averages 26–28.5%, dropping to 15–19% on bad targeting
- ~80% of those who accept never reply; a meeting comes out of 1.3% of accepts
- multichannel campaigns show 1.6–1.9x more replies, but the cohorts are self-selected. The lift is probably real and smaller than published

**The rule that removes the conflict:**

- inside a **test round** the channels are never mixed. Otherwise the verdict means nothing
- in a **live** campaign in the 2,000–10,000 zone, write in both. Email goes first, because it is unlimited. The LinkedIn slot goes to people who did not answer the first one or two touches
- record the channel of the first touch and the channel of the reply. A per-platform cut double-counts the person and lies
- planned LinkedIn capacity = profiles x 100 invites per week
- move a LinkedIn conversation into email and the CRM as soon as the person replies. A profile can be restricted, and an SDR leaves with every dialogue in it

## Choosing the sequence

Count two numbers. **N** = accounts in the ICP (TAM). **C** = monthly channel capacity.

| N | What to do |
|---|---|
| over 10,000 | Email only. LinkedIn's ceiling changes nothing at that volume, and nobody can work it by hand per account |
| 2,000–10,000 | Both channels on the same person at once. This is the multichannel zone |
| under 2,000 | ABM. Named accounts, LinkedIn from the founder, email supports. Do not run tests on this list |

Above 10,000 you have enough market to pay for learning by email and not think
about individual accounts. Under 2,000 each account costs enough to be run as its
own deal. The zone between is where the list is already expensive but not yet
named — and where the second channel recovers everyone who ignored the invite.

**Test at the bottom of the list, sell at the top.** In the 2,000–10,000 zone,
split the base by value: the bottom third is the range where you cycle segments
and offers, the top receives an angle that is already proven.

## LinkedIn-first sequence

For the ABM zone, under 2,000 accounts, where the deal is large and there is
nothing to test on.

| Step | Channel | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | LinkedIn | Manual comment on their post, if there is one. Manual, not an auto-like |
| Day 1 | LinkedIn | Invite with a 120–180 character note |
| Accept +1 | LinkedIn | Message 1: anchor and question, up to 300 characters |
| Accept +5 | LinkedIn | Message 2: a number or the asset. This is the touch that actually adds |
| Accept +10 | Email | Email to the same person, same anchor, different consequence |
| Accept +17 | LinkedIn | Close in LinkedIn |
| No accept for 14 days | — | The account moves to the email track. Withdraw the invite only if you are not returning within three weeks |

## What the 2024 playbook got wrong

| Was | 2026 verdict |
|---|---|
| Day 0: auto-likes and profile follow | Remove. Auto-warmup campaigns show 22% accept against 29.6% for plain ones. Replace with one manual comment a day before the invite: accept 27% → 44% |
| Invite and email on the same day | Remove from test rounds. Stays in live campaigns in the 2,000–10,000 zone, email first |
| 8–12 touches for the US | Remove. The same deck says 80% of replies land in the first four |
| The empty invite as a rule | Reversed. A note costs 0–4 points of accept and returns 1.5–1.9x more replies |
| SPINTAX | Remove entirely. 2026 filters look at reputation, authentication and engagement, not text overlap |
| "Neutral insight" built on someone else's article | Replace with your own asset from the same slice |
| 40 emails per day per mailbox | Down to 25–30, and only after warmup |
