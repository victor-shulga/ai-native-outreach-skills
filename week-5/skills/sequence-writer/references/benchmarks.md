# Measuring, testing, benchmarks

## The denominator comes first

Everything below is meaningless without it.

**A reply = a human reply from the addressee.** Auto-responders, "I no longer
work here" and out-of-office do not count. **The denominator is emails sent** —
not contacts, not delivered.

This matters because published benchmarks differ by a factor of eight purely
through the denominator. Belkins, on 7.5M emails, reports 0.45% (unique replies
per email sent, auto-responders excluded, open tracking off). Vendor reports show
2–3.7% and mostly do not say what they divide by.

## Benchmarks by anchor type

One benchmark for every campaign is meaningless: **1.5% is the norm for a
campaign with no anchor.** If a signal campaign returns 1.5%, it is not a signal
campaign.

| Sequence type | Replies | Positive share of all replies | Readable from |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Fresh signal, precise list | 4–8% (practitioner estimate) | 20–30% | 150 contacts |
| B. Data-point with a comparison | 2–3% | 15–25% | 300 contacts |
| C. Segment insight | 1–1.5% | up to 20% | 1,000 contacts |

Positive replies below 10% of all replies means something is broken — targeting
or the offer. Above 30% means you found something worth scaling.

A meeting comes out of roughly 0.3–0.5% of emails sent. Anyone promising
double digits is selling you something.

## List size drags conversion down

| Campaign size | Replies |
|---|---|
| under 50 contacts | ~5.8% |
| 500 and above | ~2.1% |

Source: Woodpecker, 2026; the effect repeats across several independent reports.
It also explains why "merge every segment into one campaign" always loses to
three precise ones.

## Testing

A round you cannot read is not a test.

**How much is needed for a number to mean anything.** At a 3% baseline, a 20%
lift becomes visible at roughly 1,500–2,000 emails per variant. The minimum for a
B2B comparison is 500 contacts per variant; 300 reads only as a direction.

The consequence for an agency: **subject lines are not tested on one client's
list.** The list is not big enough. Comparing on 50–100 sends is meaningless — at
a 3% baseline that is two replies against three, and the "50% difference" is noise.

**What is tested instead.** The level of "ICP x signal x offer". One hypothesis at
a time, its own campaign, its own report.

**How long.** A round runs two to six weeks. Replies arrive within 5–7 working
days of a touch, so a verdict on day two reads half the data.

**When to stop.** 500+ sends in 14 days with under 0.3% replies means the problem
is deliverability or the list. The copy is not the culprit there.

## Four things to hold in mind

1. Measure every sequence type as a separate campaign.
2. **Open rate is no longer a metric.** Apple Mail Privacy Protection loads the
   pixel regardless of whether the person opened anything, and that is half of
   all mail clients. Tracking is also disabled because the pixel damages
   deliverability.
3. Instead of opens, watch bounces, replies to the first touch, and a seed test:
   put a few of your own Gmail and Outlook mailboxes in the list and see where
   the email lands.
4. If positive replies are zero on 1,000 sends, the subject line is not the
   problem. Who you are writing to is.
